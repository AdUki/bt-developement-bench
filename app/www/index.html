Play to
          

        

          
        

        

          
          
        

        
Every stream started on this page starts on the outputs lit here, as many as you like: one stream plays to two Bluetooth speakers at once. Each running stream has its own outputs, tapped on or off while it plays. The board plays one stream of its own at a time (a test signal or a station): a new one takes the place of the one playing. Audio from devices (a phone's A2DP, a microphone) plays beside it, each a stream of its own. A speaker or car shows what plays on its display, and its buttons pause and skip it. Music from media servers is in Listen → Library.

PipeWire 

          

            
            
          

        

        
The audio stack is off, and stays off after a reboot: Bluetooth works without it — LE, GATT, the remote, files — but no device can play to or from the board (no A2DP, no HFP), and Streams and Listen have nothing to play to.

        
Restarting PipeWire and WirePlumber drops the audio links first, so devices reconnect to the new endpoints. It cures a WirePlumber that stopped answering after links vanished at once.

        
        

      

      

        
Graph

        
Each driver (a sound card, a Bluetooth transport) runs a cycle of one quantum and pulls its followers through it. Load is the cycle's busy time against the quantum; an xrun is a cycle that did not finish in time. From pw-top, a second at a time, while this is on screen.

        Filter syntax
        
The capture viewer's: type: dir: proto: handle: cid:
          psm: opcode: evt: is:err|mark|cont|lat len>100
          lat>20 (TX → NOCP, ms; such a filter shows a TX packet once its completion came, up to a second late),
          !term, a|b (either value), … || … (either filter: the quick filters combine this way, click several), and any other word or "quoted phrase" searched in the summaries.
          The board keeps the last 2048 packets while someone watches: changing the filter re-filters those, and a row
          says where packets were dropped (the page fell behind) or skipped (more matched than it sends, the newest kept).

        Filter syntax
        
Terms separated by spaces, all must match; !term negates one; | is "or" inside a value;
          a b || c matches either group; the quick filters combine this way (click several).
          type:cmd|evt|acl|sco|iso|index|log|mgmt · dir:tx|rx ·
          proto:hci|l2cap|att|smp|sdp|rfcomm|avdtp|rtp|avctp|bnep|hid|sco|iso ·
          handle:11 cid:0x40 psm:25 index:0 ·
          opcode:0x0406 (a command and its Complete/Status) evt:0x13 subevt:0x02 ·
          is:err|mark|cont|lat · len>100 n<=500 lat>20 (TX → NOCP, ms) ·
          t>=12.5 t<30 (seconds since the first packet) ·
          addr:AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF (that peer's links, and packets that name it; its first bytes are enough).
          Any other word, or a "quoted phrase",
          is searched in the summaries (case-insensitive) — slower, so on a big file the results arrive over a few seconds.
          Drag across the graph to select a time range; click it to jump there. In a packet's detail, a term under
          its summary adds it to the filter (shift-click: without it).
